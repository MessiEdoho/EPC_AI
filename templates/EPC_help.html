EPC-AI aids basic researchers in analyzing EEG datasets by predicting emergent epilepsy phenotypes. This prediction is solely based on the acute EEG recorded during status epilepticus in the intra-amygdala microinjection of kainic acid model of drug-resistant epilepsy in mice.

EPC-AI is designed to support two-channel EEG recordings sampled at 500 Hz, in both EDF and CSV formats. When uploading an EEG in EDF format, it is recommended to only upload the two-channel EEGs recorded during the status epilepticus period; if the file has more channels, you will be asked to choose two. For CSV files, upload the two-channel EEGs recorded during the status epilepticus, with the sampling frequency and the signal unit (V, mV or µV) of your EEG recordings.

Upon uploading, the system will assess the information and provide an evaluation indicating whether the EEG is more likely to exhibit normal or outlier epilepsy phenotype. Additionally, the web server offers example EEG traces from status epilepticus periods, along with the features used for prediction. These features are available for download, facilitating further analysis by researchers.

How the prediction is made

Each channel has its mean removed and 50 Hz mains noise filtered out, and is split into 5-second windows. For each window EPC-AI computes four features: the nonlinear energy of each channel, and the energy of each channel's fast ripple band (125–250 Hz, from a wavelet decomposition). Channel 1 (EEG1) should be the electrode on the side of the injection (ipsilateral) and channel 2 (EEG2) the opposite side (contralateral). The classifier labels every window as normal or outlier; the mouse is classified as outlier when 50% or more of its windows are.

The figure above shows a univariate analysis of the four features used for prediction, comparing the normal and the outlier classes within each feature across the training recordings.

We have selected an intra-amygdala kainic acid model of epilepsy (outlier) as an illustrative example (figure above). You can download sample EEG data from the 'Sample EEG Files' section on the EPC-AI home page, where available. After uploading the EEG file, please click the 'Epilepsy Phenotypes Classification' button.

Following the upload, the system will assess the information and furnish an evaluation indicating whether the EEG is more likely to exhibit normal or outlier epilepsy phenotype. The features used for prediction are available for download, enabling further analysis by researchers (by clicking 'Download the result'). The download contains the mean and standard deviation of each feature, the features, outlier probability and class of every 5-second window, and the amplitude and spectrogram of each uploaded channel for the user's in-depth analysis. Results are kept on the server for a limited time only.

The result page also shows how many windows were classified as outlier (results between 40% and 60% are marked as borderline), a timeline of the window classes over the recording, and warnings when the input looks wrong: a signal unit that does not fit EEG in volts, a recording much shorter than those the model was developed on, flat stretches that may mean a disconnected electrode, or two channels that are nearly identical.

A recording of about 30 minutes is processed in a few seconds. If users wish to apply EPC-AI with data from another mouse model, they can either conduct a preliminary test themselves or contact us at Catherine.Mooney@ucd.ie for assistance. Moreover, we will provide executables for academic use upon request from labs interested in contacting us. We are committed to aiding researchers in installing a local version of EPC-AI.
