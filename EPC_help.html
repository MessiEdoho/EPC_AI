EPC-AI aids basic researchers in analyzing EEG datasets by predicting emergent epilepsy phenotypes. This prediction is solely based on the acute EEG recorded during status epilepticus in the Intra-amygdala microinjection of kainic acid model of drug-resistant epilepsy in mice.

EPC-AI is designed to support two-channel EEG recordings in both EDF and CSV formats. When uploading an EEG in EDF format, it is recommended to only upload the two-channel EEGs recorded during the status epilepticus period. For CSV files, upload the two-channel EEGs recorded during the status epilepticus along with information on the sampling frequency of your EEG recordings.

Upon uploading, the system will assess the information and provide an evaluation indicating whether the EEG is more likely to exhibit normal or outlier epilepsy phenotype. Additionally, the web server offers example EEG traces from status epilepticus periods, along with the features used for prediction. These features are available for download, facilitating further analysis by researchers.

Figure above illustrates the univariate analysis of the multi-domain feature set. The analysis distinguishes the difference between the normal and the outlier classes within each feature. In Figure, the mean of the normal class within the nonlinear energy feature is higher than that of the outlier class. Comparably, the mean of the normal class within the fast energy ripple feature of the two channels is higher than that of the outlier class.

We have selected an Intra-amygdala kainic acid model of epilepsy (Outlier) as an illustrative example (Figure above). You can download the sample EEG data for this example from the 'Sample EEG files' section on the EPC-AI web server. The provided EDF file includes two-channel EEG recordings. After uploading the EEG file, please click the 'Epilepsy Phenotypes Classification' button.

Following the upload, the system will assess the information and furnish an evaluation indicating whether the EEG is more likely to exhibit normal or outlier epilepsy phenotype. Additionally, the web server presents sample EEG traces from status epilepticus periods, accompanied by the features used for prediction. These features are available for download, enabling further analysis by researchers (by clicking 'Download the result'). The amplitude and corresponding spectrogram of the uploaded EEG file can also be obtained by clicking 'Download the result' for the user's in-depth analysis.

This example dataset comprises approximately 20 minutes of EEG recordings. EPC-AI processes the two-channel EEG file in around 3 seconds and promptly returns the results to the user. We also provide additional examples that were used in developing EPC-AI, available for download under 'Sample EEG files.' If users wish to apply EPC-AI with data from another mouse model, they can either conduct a preliminary test themselves or contact us at Catherine.Mooney@ucd.ie for assistance. Moreover, we will provide executables for academic use upon request from labs interested in contacting us. We are committed to aiding researchers in installing a local version of EPC-AI.
